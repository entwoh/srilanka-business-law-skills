# Verification policy

This is the document that makes the difference between a useful legal knowledge base and a
confident-sounding liability.

## The hierarchy of sources

1. **The statute or regulation itself** — Act text from Parliament, gazettes from the
   Department of Government Printing.
2. **The regulator's own publication** — IRD circulars and tax charts, ROC forms and notices,
   Department of Labour gazettes, CBSL directions, DPA guidance.
3. **Reported judgments** — Sri Lanka Law Reports, New Law Reports.
4. **Professional commentary** — firm alerts, Big Four summaries, textbooks. *Useful for
   finding things. Never sufficient alone for a rate, threshold or date.*
5. **General web content** — EOR platform guides, SEO content, aggregators. *Use only as a
   lead. These are wrong constantly.*

A claim may be marked `verified` only if it rests on tier 1 or 2, or on tier 3 for a point
of interpretation.

## The four commencement questions

Before recording any legal change, answer all four:

1. **Is it a proposal or law?** Budget speeches and Cabinet decisions are neither.
2. **Is the Bill an Act?** A gazetted Bill can be amended in committee or dropped entirely.
3. **Is the Act in force?** Certification by the Speaker is not commencement. Many Sri Lankan
   Acts commence on a date the Minister appoints by gazette.
4. **Is *all* of it in force?** Parts commence separately. The PDPA has had provisions in
   force since 2023 with its central obligations still awaiting a commencement order.

## Two worked failures

### The VAT threshold that never dropped

The 2026 Budget proposed cutting the VAT registration threshold from LKR 60m to LKR 36m.
VAT (Amendment) Bill No. 65 was gazetted on 29 April 2026 containing the cut. Multiple firms
published alerts stating the new threshold applied from 1 July 2026.

The threshold cut was removed before enactment. **VAT (Amendment) Act No. 14 of 2026 kept the
threshold at LKR 60m.** Anyone who had followed the alerts would have registered a business
that had no obligation to register — and taken on monthly filing, penalties exposure and
pricing consequences for nothing.

The failure was at question 2: Bill ≠ Act.

### The 40% rate

Corporate income tax on betting, gaming, liquor and tobacco rose from 40% to 45% with effect
from 1 April 2025 (Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 2 of 2025). A large amount of online
guidance, including from otherwise credible sources, still says 40%. The failure was
staleness: no `last_verified` discipline.

## Recording a conflict

When credible sources disagree and you cannot resolve it against a primary source, **say so
in the file**. Do not pick a side silently.

```markdown
> ⚠️ **Conflicting sources.** [Source A] states X; [Source B] states Y. The Act text has not
> been checked directly. Confirm before relying on this.
```

An honest flag is more valuable than false precision. Someone will resolve it.

## Re-verification cadence

| Content | Cadence | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Tax rates, thresholds, reliefs | Quarterly | Any amendment Act; 1 April always |
| Labour figures (minimum wage, caps) | Annually | Wages Board gazettes; 1 January |
| ROC forms and fees | Twice yearly | Any ROC notice |
| Company law substance | Annually | Amendment Acts |
| Procedural steps | Annually | Regulator portal changes |
| Case law | As decided | Reported appellate decisions |

CI warns on any file whose `last_verified` is more than 180 days old.

## What "verified" obliges you to do

Marking a file `verified` means: on the date recorded, you personally checked each material
claim against a tier 1 or 2 source and it was correct. It does not mean you read the whole
Act, and it does not mean you are advising anyone. If you checked three of five figures, the
file is `partial` and you say which two you didn't check.
