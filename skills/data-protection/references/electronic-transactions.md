# Electronic records and signatures

## The Act

The **Electronic Transactions Act, No. 19 of 2006** gives legal recognition to electronic
records, communications and signatures, so that a transaction is not invalid merely because it
is in electronic form.

## What it generally enables

- Electronic records satisfying "writing" requirements
- Electronic signatures satisfying signature requirements
- Electronic records being admissible in evidence
- Contracts formed by electronic communication

Verify the precise conditions, particularly what makes an electronic signature valid and
whether different types of signature receive different treatment.

## The exclusions matter most

The Act **excludes** certain categories of transaction. Expect the exclusions to cover
instruments requiring notarial execution, and transactions concerning **land**, wills, and
similar.

**Verify the exclusion list before relying on an electronic signature for anything unusual.**
Getting this wrong produces an instrument that is void, not merely inconvenient.

## Practical position

| Document | Electronic signature |
|---|---|
| Ordinary commercial contract | Generally fine |
| NDA, service agreement, supply agreement | Generally fine |
| Employment contract | Generally fine |
| Board minutes and resolutions | Generally fine — check the Articles |
| Share transfer instrument | **Check** — stamp duty and any prescribed form |
| Land transaction | **No** — notarial execution required |
| Anything requiring notarial attestation | **No** |

## What counterparties will accept

Separate from what the law permits: banks, regulators and government departments often require
wet-ink signatures regardless of the Act. The ROC requires signed forms scanned to PDF with
visible signatures.

Check what the recipient will accept before relying on the legal position.

## Evidential practice

Where electronic signature is used, keep an audit trail: who signed, when, from what address,
and what they saw. Reputable e-signature platforms produce a certificate of completion. Keep
it with the contract.

## Relationship to data protection

E-signature platforms process personal data, often on servers outside Sri Lanka. That is a
cross-border transfer — include it in the transfer map. See
[`pdpa-obligations.md`](pdpa-obligations.md).
