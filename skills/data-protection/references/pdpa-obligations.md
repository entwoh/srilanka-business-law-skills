# PDPA obligations and readiness

## Structure of the Act

The PDPA follows the familiar controller/processor model:

- **Controller** — determines the purposes and means of processing
- **Processor** — processes on behalf of a controller
- **Data subject** — the individual the data relates to

Verify the statutory definitions, which may differ in detail from GDPR equivalents.

## Core obligations (when in force)

Verify each against the Act text, but expect:

- **Lawful basis** for processing — consent and other bases
- **Purpose limitation** — collect for specified purposes, don't repurpose freely
- **Data minimisation** — only what is necessary
- **Accuracy**
- **Storage limitation** — retention periods
- **Security** — appropriate technical and organisational measures
- **Accountability** — documented compliance
- **Transparency** — privacy notices to data subjects

## Data subject rights

Expect rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection and withdrawal of consent, together
with a procedure and timeframe for responding. Verify the specific rights and the response
deadlines in the Act.

## Special categories

Verify how the Act treats sensitive data — health, biometric, children's data — and what
additional conditions apply. Sri Lankan employers routinely hold health data, and this is
usually the first area where a business finds itself out of compliance.

## Cross-border transfers

Verify the conditions for transferring personal data outside Sri Lanka. This is directly
relevant to almost every modern business: cloud hosting, SaaS tools, offshore support, and
group companies abroad all involve transfers.

Map where your data actually sits before assuming anything.

## Breach notification

Verify whether and when notification to the Authority and to data subjects is required, and
within what timeframe.

Even before commencement, have a breach response process. The reputational and contractual
consequences of a breach do not wait for the statute.

## The Data Protection Authority

Established under the Act and operational as a regulator. It is the source of truth on
commencement, guidance and registration requirements. Check **https://www.dpa.gov.lk/**
rather than relying on secondary summaries — including this one.

## Penalties

When **Part VII** applies, administrative penalties of up to **LKR 10 million per instance** of
non-compliance with a directive, with potential doubling for repeat offences.

**Not enforceable until Part VII is commenced.** Confirm status.

## Practical readiness — worth doing regardless

These steps have value whether or not the PDPA is enforceable today, because customers,
contracts and foreign regulators already demand them:

- [ ] **Data map** — what personal data you hold, where it came from, where it sits, who can
      access it, how long you keep it
- [ ] **Privacy notice** for customers and for employees
- [ ] **Lawful basis** identified for each processing activity
- [ ] **Retention schedule**, and actual deletion
- [ ] **Security measures** — access controls, encryption, backups, MFA
- [ ] **Processor contracts** with vendors containing data protection terms
- [ ] **Transfer map** — every cloud service and offshore provider
- [ ] **Breach response plan**, with named responsibilities
- [ ] **Staff training**
- [ ] **Subject request procedure**

A business that does this is compliant-ready whenever commencement lands, and better placed to
win enterprise and European customers in the meantime.
