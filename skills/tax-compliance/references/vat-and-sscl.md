# VAT and SSCL

## ⚠️ The threshold that didn't change — read this first

The 2026 Budget proposed cutting the VAT registration threshold from **LKR 60 million** to
**LKR 36 million**. VAT (Amendment) Bill No. 65 was gazetted on **29 April 2026** containing
the cut. Professional firms published client alerts stating it took effect on 1 July 2026.

**The threshold cut was dropped before enactment.** The **VAT (Amendment) Act, No. 14 of 2026**
retained the threshold at **LKR 60 million**.

A business that acted on the alerts would have registered for VAT with no obligation to do so
— taking on monthly filing, penalty exposure and pricing consequences for nothing.

This is the reference case for [`docs/verification-policy.md`](../../../docs/verification-policy.md).
**Bill ≠ Act.**

## VAT rates

| Supply | Rate | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | **18%** | 1 January 2024 |
| **Financial services** | **20.5%** (from 18%) | Taxable periods commencing on or after 1 July 2026 |
| Exports | Zero-rated (verify scope) | |
| Exempt supplies | No VAT, no input recovery | Per the Act's schedules |

## Registration threshold

Mandatory registration where taxable supplies (other than financial services) exceed:

- **LKR 60 million** in any 12-month period, **or**
- **LKR 15 million** in a quarter (taxable period)

Application is made electronically. Verify the current application window with the IRD.

**Voluntary registration** is possible. Think carefully before doing it: it brings monthly or
quarterly filing, record-keeping obligations and penalty exposure. It is worth it if your
customers are VAT-registered businesses (who recover the input) and you have significant input
VAT to reclaim. It is usually not worth it if you sell to consumers.

## VAT on non-resident digital services

From **1 July 2026**, 18% VAT applies to services supplied by a **non-resident person through
an electronic platform** to a person in Sri Lanka.

The history is a case study in commencement risk: originally legislated by the VAT (Amendment)
Act No. 04 of 2025 to take effect 1 October 2025, **postponed twice**, and finally brought in
from 1 July 2026 by Act No. 14 of 2026.

Scope includes broad e-services, and short-term ride and accommodation platforms. Facilitating
electronic marketplaces are likely to be liable for reporting VAT on their third-party sellers.

**B2B:** where the Sri Lankan recipient is VAT-registered, the non-resident provider is **not**
required to charge or collect VAT. The provider must instead submit a **simplified statement**
to the IRD with prescribed details of those supplies. Failure to submit can attract a penalty
of up to **LKR 50,000**.

Where VAT has been charged to a Sri Lankan recipient and cannot be refunded (having been
remitted to the IRD), the VAT paid is treated as **input tax** in the hands of the registered
recipient, deductible to the extent allowable. A valid invoice from the non-resident separately
specifying the VAT constitutes a valid tax invoice.

Tax may be remitted in LKR or approved foreign currency to a designated bank account, directly
or through an appointed representative, or electronically through the IRD's online system.

**Practical effect for Sri Lankan businesses:** cloud services, SaaS subscriptions, advertising
platforms and similar become more expensive for non-registered buyers. Registered businesses
should be able to recover, subject to the input tax rules.

## Returns and payment

Monthly or quarterly depending on registration category. Verify the current filing deadlines
and the payment due dates with the IRD, and note that e-filing obligations apply.

## SVAT

The Simplified VAT scheme has long been slated for abolition, with the 2026 Budget again
announcing it. **Verify the current status** before assuming SVAT is available or withdrawn —
this has been announced and deferred more than once.

## SSCL

The **Social Security Contribution Levy** under Act No. 25 of 2022 is a separate,
turnover-based levy — not part of VAT, though the registration thresholds have historically
moved together.

Verify the current rate, the turnover threshold, and the liable categories. Budget
announcements have proposed changes to the SSCL threshold alongside VAT; confirm what was
actually enacted.

## Penalties

The VAT (Amendment) Act No. 14 of 2026 significantly increased penalties for VAT fraud and
non-compliance. Verify the current penalty schedule before advising on exposure.

## Checklist

- [ ] Turnover tested against **LKR 60m / 12 months** and **LKR 15m / quarter**
- [ ] Correct rate applied — 18% standard, 20.5% financial services from 1 July 2026
- [ ] Non-resident digital service purchases considered from 1 July 2026
- [ ] If VAT-registered and buying from non-resident providers: simplified statement position
      understood
- [ ] SSCL registration assessed separately from VAT
- [ ] SVAT status confirmed as at today
- [ ] Filing frequency and deadlines confirmed with the IRD
