# Tax filing calendar

## The year of assessment

**1 April – 31 March.**

This is the single most important fact for Sri Lankan tax timing, and the one most often
assumed away by people used to calendar-year systems. Rate changes almost always take effect
on **1 April**, at the start of a Y/A.

Companies may adopt a different financial year end, but many use **31 March** to align with
the Y/A, which simplifies everything.

## Annual

| Obligation | Deadline |
|---|---|
| **Corporate income tax return** | Within **8 months** of the financial year end. For a 31 March year end, that is 30 November. |
| Individual income tax return | Verify current deadline |
| Transfer pricing documentation | Verify, if related-party transactions exceed thresholds |

**E-filing through RAMIS / IRD e-services is mandatory for all companies.**

## Quarterly

**Income tax instalments.** From Y/A 2026/2027 these are computed as **(A − C) ÷ B** based on
the **preceding** year's liability, following abolition of the Statement of Estimated Tax.
See [`corporate-income-tax.md`](corporate-income-tax.md).

Verify the instalment due dates and the credit schedule deadlines — WHT and AIT credits must
be claimed within the prescribed windows to count against an instalment.

## Monthly

| Obligation | Notes |
|---|---|
| **VAT return and payment** | Monthly or quarterly per registration category |
| **SSCL** | Verify frequency |
| **APIT remittance** | Employer deducts and remits monthly |
| **WHT / AIT remittance** | On payments made in the month |
| **EPF and ETF** | Not tax, but same monthly rhythm — see `sl-employment-law` |

## A composite month for a typical company

A small Sri Lankan company with employees and VAT registration is dealing with, every month:

- EPF (12% + 8%) and ETF (3%) remittance
- APIT deduction and remittance
- WHT on any qualifying payments to individuals or non-residents
- VAT return and payment

Plus quarterly income tax instalments, and annually the income tax return, the ROC annual
return (Form 15) and the annual company registration levy.

It is worth setting this out for a founder budgeting for the administrative load of operating
formally. It is not trivial, and it is a common reason businesses drift into informality.

## Key dates through the year

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **1 April** | New year of assessment begins. Rate changes usually bite here. Check the new IRD tax chart. |
| **1 July** | Mid-year changes have landed here recently (2026 VAT changes) |
| **November** | Budget. **Proposals only** — do not act on them as law. |
| **30 November** | Income tax return for a 31 March year end (8 months) |
| **1 January** | Labour figures typically change (minimum wage) |
| Monthly | VAT, APIT, WHT, EPF/ETF |

## Penalties

Late filing and late payment attract penalties and interest. The VAT (Amendment) Act No. 14 of
2026 significantly increased VAT penalties in particular.

Verify current penalty rates before quantifying exposure — these change with amendments and
are among the figures most likely to be stale in secondary sources.
