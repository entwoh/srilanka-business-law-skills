# Corporate income tax

## Year of assessment

**1 April to 31 March.** Every deadline and rate change is keyed to this. A company with a
31 December financial year still reports by reference to the Y/A.

## Rates

| Category | Rate | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | **30%** | |
| Betting and gaming | **45%** | 1 April 2025 (raised from 40%) |
| Liquor and tobacco | **45%** | 1 April 2025 (raised from 40%) |
| Qualifying service exports and foreign source income | **15%** | 1 April 2025 (exemption removed) |

### The 45% rate

Raised from 40% by the **Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act, No. 2 of 2025** with effect from
**1 April 2025**. A great deal of online guidance still says 40%.

The difference is material. On taxable income of LKR 5,000,000:

| | Tax |
|---|---|
| Standard 30% | LKR 1,500,000 |
| Gaming 45% | LKR 2,250,000 |

A LKR 750,000 difference on identical profits.

### Service exports — the 2025 change

The exemption for service exports and foreign source income was **removed with effect from
1 April 2025**. That income is now taxed at **15%**, provided:

- the service is rendered to a person **to be utilised outside Sri Lanka**,
- it is **paid in foreign currency**, and
- the payment is **remitted through a bank**.

This matters enormously for Sri Lanka's IT and BPO sector, which operated tax-exempt for
years. The conditions are substantive — foreign currency received into a local rupee account
without proper routing can jeopardise the 15% treatment. Confirm the exact conditions in the
Act before structuring around it.

> ⚠️ Some sources refer to a 14% concessionary rate for exports, education and healthcare.
> That reflects an earlier position. **Verify the current concessionary rate schedule against
> the IRD tax chart for the relevant Y/A** rather than relying on any secondary summary,
> including this one.

## Quarterly instalments — the 2025/26 change

The **Statement of Estimated Tax Payable (SET) has been abolished** by the Inland Revenue
(Amendment) Act No. 2 of 2025.

From the **Year of Assessment 2026/2027**, quarterly instalments are no longer based on the
taxpayer's own estimate of the current year's profit. Each instalment is:

> **(A − C) ÷ B**
>
> where
> **A** = gross income tax payable for the **immediately preceding** year of assessment
> **B** = number of instalments remaining, including the current one (4, 3, 2, 1)
> **C** = tax already paid during the year, including WHT and AIT credits

So for Y/A 2026/2027, instalments are based on the **2025/2026** liability.

This is a significant practical change: a company whose profits fall sharply will still be
paying instalments computed on the prior year, and must manage the cash flow or seek relief.
Confirm the alternative bases and the declaration forms with the IRD.

## Capital gains

Verify the current rates — an Inland Revenue (Amendment) Bill published on **24 February 2026**
proposed increases:

- CGT for **individuals and partnerships**: 10% → **15%**
- CGT for **trusts, unit trusts, mutual funds and NGOs**: 10% → **30%**

> ⚠️ **Verify whether this Bill was enacted and commenced before relying on the increased
> rates.** A proposal published in February 2026 may or may not be law. This is exactly the
> situation where the VAT threshold error arose.

## Thin capitalisation

The Act limits the deductible financial cost for a year of assessment:

> Deductible financial cost = (financial cost of the year ÷ value of financial instruments on
> which the financial cost was incurred during the year) × 4 × (issued share capital + reserves)

The definition of "reserve" for this purpose was the subject of a 2026 amendment proposal —
previously implemented by IRD circular pending the statutory amendment. Verify the current
position.

Relevant to any company funded substantially by shareholder or related-party debt.

## Filing and payment

- Income tax return due **within 8 months of the financial year end**
- **E-filing is mandatory for all companies** through the IRD e-services portal (RAMIS)
- Quarterly instalments as above
