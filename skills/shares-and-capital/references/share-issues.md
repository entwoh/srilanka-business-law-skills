# Issuing shares

## Stated capital, not authorised capital

Sri Lanka abolished the authorised capital concept in the 2007 Act. Companies have **stated
capital** — the aggregate consideration received for shares issued. There is no ceiling to
increase and no statutory minimum.

Practical consequence: a Sri Lankan company does not need to "increase its authorised capital"
before issuing shares, unlike in jurisdictions people may be familiar with. Guidance telling
you otherwise is describing the pre-2007 position or another country.

## Authority to issue

The board generally allots shares, subject to:

- the **Articles**, which may restrict the power or reserve it to shareholders
- **pre-emption rights**, statutory or in the Articles
- any **shareholders' agreement** consent requirements
- share class rights, if there is more than one class

Verify the statutory pre-emption position under the Act and read the Articles carefully. In
companies with investors, a new issue is almost always a reserved matter requiring investor
consent.

## Pre-emption

Where pre-emption applies, existing shareholders must be offered the opportunity to take up
new shares in proportion to their holdings before the shares go to an outsider.

To proceed without running the full process, obtain **written waivers** from every shareholder
entitled to pre-emption. Verbal agreement at a meeting is not enough — get signatures, keep
them with the minute.

An allotment in breach of pre-emption is challengeable by the prejudiced shareholder, and the
issue is often discovered years later during diligence.

## Consideration

Shares may be issued for cash or for other consideration. Non-cash consideration (services,
assets, conversion of a loan) needs care:

- the board should be satisfied the consideration is fair and record why
- valuation should be documented
- converting director loans to equity is common and should be minuted precisely
- non-cash consideration has tax consequences for both sides

## Mechanics

1. **Check the Articles** — power to allot, pre-emption, class rights
2. **Check any shareholders' agreement** — consent thresholds
3. **Amend the Articles if needed** — special resolution + **Form 39**. Do this *before* the
   allotment if the new shares need rights the Articles don't currently provide.
4. **Pre-emption**: run the offer or collect written waivers
5. **Subscription documentation** — a subscription or share purchase agreement for anything
   other than a trivial issue
6. **Foreign investor**: confirm the **Inward Investment Account** route *before* funds move
7. **Receive the money**
8. **Board resolution** allotting the shares, recording consideration and the number and class
9. **Update the register of shareholders**
10. **Issue share certificates**
11. **File Form 6** — Notice of Issue of Shares
12. **Update beneficial ownership** if the new holder reaches 10% or more, or if the issue
    changes anyone else's position across that line
13. Reflect in the next **annual return (Form 15)**

## Form 6

**Form 6 is the Notice of Issue of Shares.** It is available from the ROC forms page. Confirm
the filing deadline under the Act — it is short, and late filing is a default.

Secondary guidance frequently misnames this form. The ROC's own list is the authority; see
[`../../shared/roc-forms-index.md`](../../../shared/roc-forms-index.md).

## Classes of shares

A company may issue different classes with different rights — voting, dividend, liquidation
preference, redemption. The Articles must provide for the class and its rights.

Investor rounds commonly want preference shares with liquidation preference and anti-dilution.
If the current Articles only contemplate ordinary shares, amend them first. Retrofitting class
rights after the money has come in is difficult and sometimes requires shareholder consent
that is no longer easy to obtain.

## Stamp duty

Stamp duty generally attaches to **transfers** rather than issues, but verify the current
position under the Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act No. 12 of 2006 and applicable
regulations, since the treatment of instruments changes.

## Foreign investors

Before any money moves:

- confirm the sector permits the intended foreign shareholding percentage
- open or confirm an **Inward Investment Account** at a licensed bank
- ensure funds arrive through that account and are documented as investment

Getting this wrong does not usually stop the investment — it stops the **exit**. Repatriating
capital and dividends later depends on the inflow having been properly routed and recorded.

See [`../../foreign-investment/references/exchange-control.md`](../../foreign-investment/references/exchange-control.md).

## Closing checklist

- [ ] Articles checked; amended by special resolution + Form 39 if needed
- [ ] Shareholders' agreement consents obtained
- [ ] Pre-emption run or waived in writing by every entitled holder
- [ ] Subscription agreement executed
- [ ] Foreign funds routed through an IIA
- [ ] Funds actually received before allotment
- [ ] Board resolution passed and minuted
- [ ] Register of shareholders updated
- [ ] Share certificates issued
- [ ] **Form 6 filed within the deadline**
- [ ] Beneficial ownership register and ROC filing updated
- [ ] Cap table reconciled against the register
