# Distributions, solvency test, buy-backs and capital reduction

## The solvency test governs all of these

Sri Lanka follows the modern approach: rather than rigid capital maintenance rules, the
Companies Act No. 7 of 2007 makes a **solvency test** the gateway to any return of value to
shareholders.

The test has two limbs, both of which must be satisfied:

1. **Cash flow:** the company is able to pay its debts as they become due in the normal course
   of business; **and**
2. **Balance sheet:** the value of the company's assets is greater than the value of its
   liabilities, including contingent liabilities.

Verify the precise statutory wording and the certification mechanics — typically the directors
must be satisfied on reasonable grounds and sign a certificate.

**Directors who certify wrongly are personally exposed.** This is not a formality.

## Dividends

A distribution to shareholders requires the solvency test to be satisfied. Verify:

- who authorises a distribution (board, and any Articles requirements)
- the certification requirement and its form
- the timing relationship between certification and payment
- treatment of interim versus final dividends

**Tax:** dividends from a Sri Lankan resident company attract withholding. Confirm the current
rate and exemptions with [`../../tax-compliance/references/withholding.md`](../../tax-compliance/references/withholding.md).

**Foreign shareholders:** repatriating a dividend depends on the original investment having
been routed through an **Inward Investment Account**. If it wasn't, this is where the problem
appears. Check before promising anyone a dividend.

## Share buy-backs and redemption

A company acquiring or redeeming its own shares must satisfy the solvency test and follow the
statutory procedure.

**ROC filing: Form 9** — Notice of acquisition or redemption by company of own shares.

Verify:
- authorisation required (board, ordinary resolution, special resolution?)
- whether offers must be made to all shareholders or may be selective
- treatment of the shares acquired — cancelled, or held?
- effect on stated capital

Buy-backs are commonly used to exit a departing founder or a minority shareholder. The
alternative — another shareholder buying the shares personally — is often simpler and avoids
the solvency test entirely. Consider both.

## Reduction of stated capital

**ROC filing: Form 8** — Notice of reduction of stated capital.

Verify the procedure, which will involve a resolution and satisfaction of the solvency test,
and may involve creditor protection. Reductions are relatively unusual and warrant advice.

## Where value gets returned informally — the real risk

In practice, owner-managed Sri Lankan companies frequently return value through routes that
avoid the distribution rules entirely: director loans, above-market salaries, company-paid
personal expenses, and rent to related parties.

These have their own consequences:

- **Tax:** deductibility challenges, and transfer pricing for related-party dealings
- **Company law:** interested-party transaction rules, and potentially a disguised distribution
- **Investors:** almost always prohibited by a shareholders' agreement
- **Insolvency:** vulnerable to challenge if the company later fails

A company with outside shareholders or lenders should be returning value through declared,
documented distributions.
