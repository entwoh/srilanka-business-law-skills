# Shareholder agreements

## Agreement versus Articles

This distinction decides most disputes about which document wins.

| | **Articles of Association** | **Shareholders' agreement** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | The company's constitution | A contract between the parties |
| Binds | The company and all shareholders, including future ones | Only the parties who signed |
| Public | Filed with the ROC | Private |
| Amended by | Special resolution + Form 39 | Unanimous consent of parties (usually) |
| Remedy for breach | Company law remedies | Contractual — damages, specific performance |

**The practical rule:** anything that must bind a future shareholder or a third party belongs
in the **Articles**. Anything commercially sensitive, or that the parties want to keep private
and easy to amend, belongs in the **agreement**.

The most common Sri Lankan drafting failure is putting investor protections only in the
shareholders' agreement and never making the conforming amendments to the Articles. The
protections then fail exactly when they're needed — against a transferee who never signed.

## What a shareholders' agreement should cover

### Governance
- Board composition and appointment rights
- **Reserved matters** requiring investor or supermajority consent — new share issues, debt
  above a threshold, related-party transactions, changing the business, disposing of assets,
  winding up
- Information rights: management accounts, audited accounts, board papers
- Meeting frequency and quorum requirements

### Share transfers
- **Pre-emption** on transfer
- **Tag-along** — minority can join a majority sale on the same terms
- **Drag-along** — majority can compel minority to sell, preventing a hold-out
- Permitted transfers (to affiliates, family, trusts)
- Lock-in periods for founders
- Good leaver / bad leaver treatment of founder shares

### Economics
- Dividend policy
- Anti-dilution protection
- Liquidation preference (which needs a share class in the **Articles**)
- Valuation mechanism for compulsory transfers

### Founders
- Vesting or reverse vesting
- Full-time commitment
- Non-compete and non-solicit — draft these carefully, since restraints must be reasonable in
  scope, duration and geography to be enforceable
- IP assignment to the company

### Exit and deadlock
- Exit intentions and timeframe
- Drag mechanics on a trade sale
- Deadlock resolution: escalation, then a buy-sell mechanism ("Russian roulette" / "Texas
  shoot-out") or a casting vote
- Dispute resolution — see below

### Boilerplate that matters
- Governing law: Sri Lankan law
- Dispute resolution: **arbitration is usually preferable** to the courts for shareholder
  disputes — faster, private, and enforceable internationally under the New York Convention
  via the Arbitration Act No. 11 of 1995
- Confidentiality
- Which document prevails on inconsistency with the Articles (note this cannot override the
  Articles as against third parties)

## Sri Lanka-specific points

**Conforming amendments to the Articles.** Budget for a special resolution and a Form 39 as
part of closing. Do it at closing, not "soon after".

**Beneficial ownership.** Any shareholder crossing 10% triggers the BO register and ROC
filing. Build it into the closing checklist.

**Foreign investors.** The agreement should record that investment comes through an Inward
Investment Account, because the exit provisions depend on repatriation being possible.

**Nominee directors.** A director appointed under the agreement still owes duties to the
company, not to their appointer. Reserved-matter mechanics protect the investor better than
relying on the nominee's vote. See
[`../../company-secretarial/references/directors-duties.md`](../../company-secretarial/references/directors-duties.md).

**Stamp duty and tax** on any transfer triggered by the agreement — drag, tag, or compulsory
transfer — should be considered when the mechanism is drafted, not when it fires.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does anything in the agreement need to be in the Articles to bind transferees?
- [ ] Are the Articles being amended at closing, with a Form 39?
- [ ] Do share classes in the Articles support the economics in the agreement?
- [ ] Are reserved matters specific enough to be workable, and short enough not to paralyse?
- [ ] Is there a deadlock mechanism that actually terminates?
- [ ] Is the valuation mechanism objective and capable of being run without cooperation?
- [ ] Are restraints on founders reasonable enough to be enforceable?
- [ ] Is dispute resolution by arbitration, with a clear seat and rules?
- [ ] Is the BO filing on the closing checklist?
- [ ] For foreign investors: is the IIA routing documented?
