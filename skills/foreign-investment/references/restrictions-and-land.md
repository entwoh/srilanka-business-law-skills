# Sectoral restrictions, land and Port City

## Foreign ownership of Sri Lankan companies

The general position is permissive — **100% foreign ownership is available in most sectors**.
But there are three categories to check:

| Category | Position |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Up to 100% foreign ownership permitted |
| **Restricted / capped** | Foreign ownership permitted only up to a specified percentage |
| **Closed or approval-required** | Prohibited, or requires specific government approval |

Historically restricted or regulated areas have included: certain retail and trading
activities, some services reserved for nationals, and sectors regulated for other reasons
(banking, insurance, broadcasting, security services, defence-related activity, coastal
fishing, and some land-related activity).

**The list is set by gazette and changes.** Do not advise a percentage from memory or from an
undated source. Confirm the current position before anyone commits.

Sector-specific regulators may impose their own ownership conditions on top of the general
position — a licensed finance company or an insurer answers to the CBSL or the IRCSL as well.

## Land

The **Land (Restrictions on Alienation) Act, No. 38 of 2014** restricts the transfer of land in
Sri Lanka to:

- foreign nationals,
- foreign companies, and
- **Sri Lankan companies with foreign shareholding above a specified threshold**.

That third limb is the one that catches people. A wholly Sri Lankan company that later takes
foreign investment can cross the threshold and find its land-holding position has changed.
If a company owns land and is contemplating foreign investment, deal with this **before** the
investment, not after.

Leases are treated differently from freehold transfers, and have been the standard workaround,
subject to their own conditions and duration limits. Verify the current rules, including any
applicable lease tax.

**Land is out of scope for this project generally.** It requires a Notary Public, an
Attorney-at-Law, and specialist advice. The point of this section is only to flag that foreign
investment and land interact, and that the interaction is a trap.

## Colombo Port City

The **Colombo Port City Economic Commission Act, No. 11 of 2021**, amended in 2026, creates a
special economic zone with its own regime covering:

- tax treatment
- exchange control
- employment
- business licensing, through the Port City Economic Commission

**Do not assume mainland Sri Lankan rules apply inside Port City, or vice versa.** It is a
genuinely separate regime, and answering a Port City question from general Sri Lankan law will
produce a wrong answer.

The 2026 amendment addressed, among other things, businesses of strategic importance within
the zone. Verify the current framework and the designation criteria before advising.

Worth considering for: regional headquarters, financial services, and businesses whose
customers and revenue are primarily offshore.
