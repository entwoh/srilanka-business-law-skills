# Exchange control and Inward Investment Accounts

## The governing framework

The **Foreign Exchange Act, No. 12 of 2017** replaced the older, stricter Exchange Control Act.
The current regime is liberalised by comparison, but capital transactions remain regulated and
the detail sits in regulations and CBSL directions rather than the Act itself.

**The licensed commercial bank is the practical gatekeeper**, not the Central Bank. Banks
administer the accounts, apply the rules, and are the ones who will refuse an outward
remittance if the inward documentation is inadequate.

## The Inward Investment Account (IIA)

The IIA is the designated account through which foreign investment into Sri Lanka should flow.

**Why it matters:** the right to repatriate capital, capital gains and dividends out of Sri
Lanka depends on the investment having come **in** through the correct channel, properly
documented as investment.

The failure mode is consistent and predictable:

1. A foreign investor sends money to the company's ordinary account, or to a director
   personally, because it was faster
2. Shares are issued
3. Years later, the investor wants to exit or take a dividend
4. The bank asks for evidence the funds came in as foreign investment through an IIA
5. There is none
6. Repatriation becomes difficult, slow, or impossible

Fixing this retrospectively is hard. **Open the account first.**

## Practical sequence

1. Investor (or the Sri Lankan company, depending on structure) opens the IIA at a licensed bank
2. Bank completes KYC on the investor
3. Funds remitted from abroad into the IIA, with the purpose documented as investment
4. Funds moved from the IIA to the company as subscription money
5. Shares issued; **Form 6** filed
6. All documentation retained permanently — bank advices, board resolutions, share
   certificates, Form 6 acknowledgement

## What must be documented

- Inward remittance advice showing the source and the purpose
- Board resolution allotting the shares
- Subscription or share purchase agreement
- Share certificates
- Form 6 filing
- Register of shareholders entry

Keep all of it indefinitely. The person asking for it in ten years will not accept
reconstruction.

## Repatriation

Subject to the current regulations, an investor who came in properly can generally repatriate:

- **Dividends** — after tax and withholding
- **Sale proceeds** on disposal of the shares
- **Capital**, on a return of capital or liquidation

Verify current conditions, documentation requirements, and any restrictions in force. Sri Lanka
has imposed temporary limits during balance-of-payments stress, and the position can change.

## Foreign loans

Foreign borrowing by a Sri Lankan company has its own treatment — permissions, registration,
and conditions on interest and repayment. Verify before drawing down. Do not assume a
shareholder loan from a foreign parent is unregulated; it usually is not.
