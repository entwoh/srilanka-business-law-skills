# Winding up and insolvency

## Routes

| Route | When |
|---|---|
| **Members' voluntary winding up** | Solvent company, shareholders decide to close. Requires a **declaration of solvency (Form 28)**. |
| **Creditors' voluntary winding up** | Company cannot pay its debts; members initiate |
| **Winding up by the court** | On a petition, commonly by a creditor for inability to pay debts |
| **Administration** | Where an administrator is appointed (Form 34) |
| **Strike off / removal** | Where a company is defunct — verify the current ROC procedure |

The Official Receiver Unit at the ROC administers aspects of winding up; the ROC publishes the
Winding Up Rules.

## As a creditor

### The statutory demand route

Where the debt is **undisputed**, a statutory demand followed by a winding-up petition is
effective leverage — a company that can pay usually does rather than face winding up.

**Do not use it for a disputed debt.** A petition based on a genuinely disputed debt can be an
abuse of process, with costs consequences.

Verify the current statutory demand threshold and the period for compliance.

### If the company really is insolvent

You become one creditor among many. Realistically:

- **Secured creditors** are paid from their security first
- **Preferential claims** — verify the categories, which typically include employee
  entitlements and certain taxes
- **Unsecured creditors** share what remains, usually a fraction
- **Shareholders** last, usually nothing

Petitioning costs money and you may fund a process that returns you little. Assess the asset
position before petitioning.

### Protect yourself in advance

The time to deal with counterparty insolvency is at contract stage, not at petition stage:

- Retention of title on goods
- Security over assets
- Personal guarantees from directors
- Advance payments
- Credit limits actively monitored

See [`debt-recovery.md`](debt-recovery.md).

## As a director of a company in difficulty

**Take advice early.** Directors' exposure increases as a company approaches insolvency:

- **Reckless trading** — carrying on business in a way likely to create substantial risk of
  serious loss to creditors
- **Incurring obligations** the company cannot reasonably be expected to perform
- Unpaid **EPF/ETF** — officers can be pursued
- **Tax** defaults
- Transactions that may be challenged as preferences or undervalue

Continuing to trade while hoping things improve is the decision that converts a company failure
into personal liability. See
[`../../company-secretarial/references/directors-duties.md`](../../company-secretarial/references/directors-duties.md).

## Closing a solvent company properly

A company that has finished trading should be wound up or removed, not abandoned. An abandoned
company continues to accrue:

- Annual return defaults — with **personal fines for directors**
- Annual company registration levy
- Tax filing obligations

Directors who have mentally moved on remain of record and exposed. Members' voluntary winding
up, with a declaration of solvency (Form 28), is the clean route.
