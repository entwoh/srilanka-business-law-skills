# Debt recovery

## Before you do anything

1. **Check prescription.** An old invoice may be unenforceable.
2. **Confirm the debt is undisputed.** Summary procedures are for clear debts. A genuine
   dispute about quality, delivery or set-off will take it out of the summary route.
3. **Check the debtor's solvency.** Pursuing a company with no assets wastes money.
4. **Read the contract** — interest on late payment, dispute resolution clause, notice
   requirements.

## Escalation ladder

### 1. Commercial follow-up

Before anything legal. Invoice reminders, a call, a payment plan. Most late payment is cash
flow, not refusal, and a structured payment plan recovers more than litigation does.

Get any payment arrangement **in writing** — it acknowledges the debt, which may interrupt
prescription.

### 2. Letter of demand

From an Attorney-at-Law. Should state the debt, its basis, the amount, a deadline, and the
consequence of non-payment.

This resolves a substantial proportion of cases, because it signals the creditor is serious
and creates a record.

### 3. Debt Recovery (Special Provisions) Act No. 2 of 1990

A **summary procedure** designed to be faster than ordinary civil action.

**Verify carefully who may use it and for what.** It has historically been associated with
lenders and institutional creditors, and there may be limits on the class of creditor and the
nature of the debt. Do not assume an ordinary trade creditor can use it without checking.

Where available, it is materially faster than ordinary procedure.

### 4. Ordinary civil action

District Court or Commercial High Court depending on value and subject matter. Slow. See
[`courts-and-procedure.md`](courts-and-procedure.md).

### 5. Winding up as leverage

Where the debtor is a company and the debt is undisputed, a statutory demand followed by a
winding-up petition can be effective — a solvent company facing winding up usually pays.

**Two cautions:**
- It is **not** a debt collection tool for a **disputed** debt. Using it that way can be an
  abuse of process with costs consequences.
- If the company really is insolvent, you become one creditor among many, ranking behind
  secured and preferential claims. You may recover nothing while having funded the process.

See [`insolvency.md`](insolvency.md).

## Interest

Check the contract for a late payment interest provision. Without one, verify what interest is
recoverable at law and from what date. A contractual interest clause is much easier to enforce
than a claim for interest at large — include one in your standard terms.

## Security — prevention beats recovery

The cheapest debt recovery is the one you never need:

- Credit checks before extending terms
- Advance payments or deposits
- Personal guarantees from directors of small corporate customers
- Retention of title clauses on goods
- Security over assets for significant exposure
- Letters of credit or bank guarantees for export
- Clear payment terms with interest on late payment
- Stopping supply promptly when an account goes overdue

A retention of title clause and a director's guarantee cost nothing at contract stage and
change the recovery position entirely.
