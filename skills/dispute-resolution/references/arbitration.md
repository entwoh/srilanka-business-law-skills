# Arbitration

## Why it usually beats litigation for commercial disputes

The **Arbitration Act, No. 11 of 1995** governs domestic and international arbitration in
Sri Lanka.

| | Arbitration | Litigation |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Faster, and within the parties' control | Slow — years |
| Privacy | Private | Public |
| Decision-maker | Chosen, and can be a specialist | Assigned |
| Procedure | Flexible, agreed by the parties | Fixed by the Civil Procedure Code |
| Appeals | Very limited | Available, and extend timelines considerably |
| **Cross-border enforcement** | **New York Convention** | Much harder |
| Cost | Arbitrator and institutional fees payable | Court fees lower, but duration costs more |

For cross-border contracts, the enforcement point is decisive. An arbitral award is enforceable
in the many New York Convention states; a Sri Lankan court judgment is considerably harder to
enforce abroad, and vice versa.

## Drafting the clause

An incomplete arbitration clause creates a preliminary dispute about how to resolve the
dispute — the worst outcome. Specify:

- [ ] **That disputes are referred to arbitration** — and the scope ("any dispute arising out
      of or in connection with this agreement")
- [ ] **Seat** of the arbitration (Colombo, Singapore, London) — this determines the supervising
      court and the procedural law, and is not the same as the venue of hearings
- [ ] **Rules** — institutional (SLNAC, SIAC, ICC, LCIA) or ad hoc (UNCITRAL)
- [ ] **Number of arbitrators** — one for smaller disputes, three for large
- [ ] **Appointing authority** if the parties cannot agree
- [ ] **Language**
- [ ] **Governing law** of the contract, stated separately from the seat

For most Sri Lankan commercial contracts, a sole arbitrator seated in Colombo under
institutional rules is proportionate. For significant cross-border contracts, a neutral seat
such as Singapore is common and often easier to agree.

## Effect of an arbitration agreement

Where a valid arbitration agreement exists and a party nonetheless sues, the court will
generally **stay** the court proceedings and hold the parties to their bargain. Verify the
procedure and any time limit for applying for a stay — it is usually early, and a party that
takes a step in the proceedings may lose the right.

## Procedure, in outline

1. Notice of arbitration
2. Constitution of the tribunal
3. Preliminary meeting — timetable and procedure
4. Written submissions and documentary evidence
5. Hearing, with witnesses and experts as needed
6. Award

Timelines are what the parties and the tribunal make them, which is the principal practical
advantage.

## Interim measures

Verify the tribunal's power to grant interim relief, and the court's supporting powers —
particularly where relief is needed before the tribunal is constituted.

## Enforcement

**Domestic awards:** verify the procedure for recognition and enforcement, and the limited
grounds on which an award can be set aside — typically procedural irregularity, lack of
jurisdiction, or public policy, not error of law or fact.

**Foreign awards:** Sri Lanka is a party to the **New York Convention**, so foreign arbitral
awards are enforceable subject to the Convention's limited grounds of refusal. This is the
single strongest argument for arbitration in any contract with a foreign counterparty.

## When arbitration is not the right choice

- **Small claims** — arbitrator and institutional fees can exceed the amount in dispute
- **Undisputed debts** — summary recovery routes are faster and cheaper
- Where **urgent injunctive relief** against a third party is the real objective
- Where you actually want a **public** determination, or a precedent

## Mediation

Worth an escalation tier before arbitration. Cheap relative to both alternatives, preserves
commercial relationships, and settles a high proportion of disputes.

A tiered clause — negotiation between senior executives, then mediation, then arbitration —
works well, provided each tier has a clear time limit so it cannot be used to delay.
