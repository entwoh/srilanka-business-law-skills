# Courts, procedure and enforcement

## Court structure, in outline

| Court | Role |
|---|---|
| **Supreme Court** | Final appellate court; constitutional and fundamental rights jurisdiction |
| **Court of Appeal** | Appeals; writ jurisdiction |
| **High Court** | Including the **Commercial High Court** (Western Province, Colombo) for commercial matters |
| **District Court** | General civil jurisdiction |
| **Magistrate's Court** | Criminal, and limited civil |
| **Labour Tribunals** | Individual employment disputes — see `sl-employment-law` |

## Commercial High Court

The High Court of the Western Province exercising commercial jurisdiction, sitting in Colombo,
handles significant commercial disputes, including company matters and intellectual property.

**Verify the current monetary threshold** for its jurisdiction and the categories of matter it
hears — these have been adjusted over time.

Advantages over the District Court: judges with commercial experience, and procedures adapted
to commercial disputes.

## Prescription

**Verify the applicable period under the Prescription Ordinance before anything else.**
Different classes of claim carry different periods, and some are shorter than parties assume.

Time generally runs from when the cause of action arises. Acknowledgement of a debt may
restart it — which is why a partial payment or a written acknowledgement from a debtor is
valuable and should be obtained where possible.

## Timelines — be honest with clients

Sri Lankan civil litigation is slow. Delays arise from listing, postponements, interlocutory
applications and appeals. A commercial case reaching final judgment in under two years would
be quick; several years is common; appeals extend it further.

Plan accordingly:
- Interim relief may matter more than final judgment
- Settlement leverage decays as costs accumulate on both sides
- A defendant with no incentive to resolve can use delay as a strategy

## Interim relief

Where assets may be dissipated or harm is ongoing, interim relief — injunction or enjoining
order — may be available. This is urgent and evidence-heavy.

**Do not alert the defendant while you gather evidence.** Instruct an Attorney-at-Law
immediately; the application often needs to be made before the other side knows.

## Costs

Sri Lankan courts award costs, but recovery is typically well short of actual legal spend.
Budget on the basis that you will bear most of your own costs even if you win.

## Enforcement

**Plan enforcement before you sue.** A judgment is only as good as the assets behind it.

Before commencing, establish:
- Does the defendant have assets in Sri Lanka?
- Are they encumbered?
- Is the company a shell with the real assets held elsewhere?
- Are there directors or guarantors with personal exposure?

Enforcement methods include seizure and sale of property, and garnishee-type orders against
third parties holding money for the debtor. Verify the mechanisms and procedure under the
Civil Procedure Code.

## Foreign judgments

Verify the basis on which a foreign judgment may be recognised and enforced in Sri Lanka — it
is generally more restrictive than for arbitral awards, which is one of the strongest arguments
for an arbitration clause in cross-border contracts. See [`arbitration.md`](arbitration.md).
