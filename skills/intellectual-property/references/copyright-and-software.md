# Copyright and software

## Copyright arises automatically

No registration is required. Copyright subsists in original literary, artistic, musical and
dramatic works, and in computer programs, from creation.

Sri Lanka is party to international copyright conventions, so Sri Lankan works receive
protection in member states and vice versa. Verify the current treaty position.

## Software

**Computer programs are protected as literary works.** This covers source code and object code.

What copyright does *not* protect: the **idea**, the functionality, or the algorithm as such.
A competitor who independently writes software doing the same thing does not infringe. Copying
your code does.

For functional innovation, consider patents (subject to what is patentable) and, more
realistically for most software businesses, **trade secrets** plus contractual protection.

## Ownership — the critical issue

This is where businesses lose their IP.

**Employees.** Work created in the course of employment generally vests in the employer, but
**verify the exact statutory provision** and its conditions. Do not rely on the default —
include an express IP assignment in every employment contract.

**Contractors and agencies.** Commissioning and paying for a work does **not** automatically
make you the owner. Without a written assignment, the contractor may retain copyright and you
may have only an implied licence — possibly a narrow one.

This affects, constantly:
- Logos and brand assets from a design freelancer
- Websites from an agency
- Mobile apps and source code from a development shop
- Photography and video
- Content and copy

**Founders.** Code written by a founder before incorporation belongs to the founder personally
unless assigned to the company. Investors will ask. Assign it at incorporation.

## Moral rights

The author retains moral rights — attribution and integrity — which typically cannot be
assigned, though they may be waived. Verify the Sri Lankan position, which affects what an
assignment can actually achieve.

## Duration

Verify the terms under the IP Act. Generally the author's life plus a period for literary and
artistic works, with different rules for corporate and anonymous works.

## Assignments and licences

An assignment should be:

- **In writing and signed**
- Specific about the works covered — including future works where permitted
- Clear that it is an assignment, not a licence
- Supported by consideration
- Accompanied by a waiver of moral rights where permissible
- Accompanied by an obligation to execute further documents if needed

For a licence, specify: scope, exclusivity, territory, duration, sublicensing, and what happens
on termination.

## Practical audit

Any business preparing for investment or sale should be able to answer:

- [ ] Who wrote every material piece of our code?
- [ ] Do we have a signed assignment from each of them?
- [ ] Do employment contracts include IP assignment?
- [ ] Do contractor agreements include IP assignment?
- [ ] Did founders assign pre-incorporation work to the company?
- [ ] Do we have rights to the logo and brand assets?
- [ ] What open source is in the codebase, and do we comply with its licences?
- [ ] Do we have licences for the third-party content we use?

Gaps are far cheaper to fix now, while everyone is still on good terms, than during a
transaction when the counterparty has leverage.
