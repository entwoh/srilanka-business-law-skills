# Trade marks

## What can be registered

Marks capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one enterprise from those of another
— words, names, logos, devices, and combinations.

Generally **not** registrable: marks that are purely descriptive of the goods, generic terms,
marks likely to deceive, marks contrary to public order or morality, state emblems, and marks
conflicting with earlier rights.

Verify the specific grounds for refusal in Part V of the IP Act.

## Company name ≠ trade mark

Registering a company name with the ROC does **not** give trade mark rights. They are separate
systems with separate registers and separate purposes.

A business can hold a company name it cannot use as a brand, because someone else registered
the trade mark. Check both before committing to a name.

## Classes

Registration is by class of goods and services under the Nice Classification. Protection
extends only to the classes registered.

Practical points:
- File in every class where you trade **and** where you realistically will
- A software company may need classes covering software, SaaS services, and possibly
  merchandising
- Adding a class later means a new application, with a later priority date

## Procedure, in outline

1. **Search** the register first. Do this before spending money on branding.
2. **File** the application at NIPO with the mark, the applicant details, and the classes
3. **Examination** for absolute and relative grounds
4. **Publication** in the IP gazette
5. **Opposition period** — third parties may oppose. Verify the period.
6. **Registration** and certificate

Verify realistic timelines with NIPO. Sri Lankan trade mark registration is not fast.

## Duration and renewal

Registration runs for a term and is renewable indefinitely. **Verify the term and the renewal
window**, and diarise it — lapsed marks are lost and re-registration may not be possible if
someone else has moved in.

## Use requirements

Verify whether the Act provides for revocation on grounds of non-use, and over what period.
Most systems do. A defensive registration you never use may be vulnerable.

## Unregistered marks

Sri Lanka recognises unfair competition and passing-off style protections, and well-known
marks may receive protection without registration under the IP Act.

But unregistered protection is harder, slower and more expensive to enforce than a
registration. Register.

## International protection

Sri Lankan registration protects **in Sri Lanka only**. For other markets, file there.

Verify Sri Lanka's current position on the Madrid Protocol — if it is a member, international
filing is simpler; if not, national filings are needed in each country of interest.

## Enforcement

Infringement proceedings go to the **Commercial High Court**. Remedies typically include
injunctions, damages or an account of profits, and delivery up of infringing goods.

Customs recordal can help against counterfeit imports; verify the current procedure.

## Checklist

- [ ] Search conducted before branding investment
- [ ] All relevant classes identified, present and future
- [ ] Applicant is the right entity — the **company**, not a founder personally
- [ ] Renewal diarised
- [ ] Foreign filings considered for export markets
- [ ] Any licence of the mark documented in writing
