# Post-incorporation setup

A certificate of incorporation does not let you trade, bank or hire. This is the work that does.

## Immediate (first two weeks)

### 1. Beneficial ownership

In force since **30 March 2026**. New companies are caught from incorporation — there is no
grace period for new incorporations. Identify beneficial owners (natural persons owning or
controlling **10% or more**, or exercising effective control), open the BO register, and
appoint an authorised person resident in Sri Lanka (Form BO 5).

Non-compliance carries criminal exposure for officers personally. See
[`../../company-secretarial/references/beneficial-ownership.md`](../../company-secretarial/references/beneficial-ownership.md).

### 2. Share certificates and statutory registers

Issue share certificates. Open the registers of shareholders, directors, secretaries and
charges, and start a minute book. See
[`../../company-secretarial/references/registers-and-records.md`](../../company-secretarial/references/registers-and-records.md).

### 3. Company seals and stamps

Company stamp, plus director and secretary stamps. Banks and the ROC expect them.

### 4. TIN from the IRD

The **Taxpayer Identification Number** is the gateway. Banks want it, the BOI wants it,
EPF registration follows it. Apply as soon as the certificate is downloadable.

## Within the first month

### 5. Corporate bank account

Banks typically want: certificate of incorporation, certified Form 1, certified Articles,
certified Form 18/19, TIN, board resolution authorising the account and naming signatories,
and KYC on every director and significant shareholder.

**If there are foreign shareholders**, the account structure matters enormously. Foreign
investment should come in through an **Inward Investment Account (IIA)** if the investor
wants to repatriate capital and dividends freely later. Getting this wrong at the start is
painful to unwind. See
[`../../foreign-investment/references/exchange-control.md`](../../foreign-investment/references/exchange-control.md).

### 6. Tax registrations

| Registration | When required |
|---|---|
| Income tax (via TIN) | Always |
| **VAT** | Once taxable supplies exceed the threshold — **LKR 60m per 12 months, or LKR 15m per quarter**. Voluntary registration is possible. |
| **SSCL** | Turnover-based; check the current threshold |
| **APIT** | As soon as you have employees earning above the relief threshold |
| WHT/AIT | If making payments subject to withholding |

> ⚠️ Do not rely on the widely-reported LKR 36m VAT threshold. The proposed reduction was
> **dropped before enactment**. The threshold remains LKR 60m. See
> [`../../tax-compliance/references/vat-and-sscl.md`](../../tax-compliance/references/vat-and-sscl.md).

### 7. EPF and ETF registration

If you have even one employee: register as an employer for **EPF** (Labour Department) and
**ETF** (ETF Board). Contributions run from the first day of employment — there is no
probationary exemption.

| Fund | Employer | Employee |
|---|---|---|
| EPF | 12% | 8% |
| ETF | 3% | — |

Late contributions attract surcharges. See
[`../../employment-law/references/epf-etf-gratuity.md`](../../employment-law/references/epf-etf-gratuity.md).

## Within the first quarter

### 8. Sector licences and permits

Depending on activity: local authority trade licence, Central Environmental Authority
approval for manufacturing, food handling registration, CBSL licence for financial services,
SEC registration, telecoms or broadcasting licences, tourism registration.

Some of these are prerequisites to trading, not formalities. Identify them early.

### 9. Accounting system and balance sheet date

Adopt a balance sheet date and notify it if required (Form 17 governs adoption or change).
Sri Lankan companies commonly use 31 March to align with the year of assessment, but this is
a choice with consequences — discuss with your accountant.

Appoint auditors if required.

### 10. Employment documentation

Written particulars of employment for every employee, EPF/ETF enrolment, and a leave and
attendance system that satisfies the Shop and Office Employees Act. See
[`../../employment-law/SKILL.md`](../../employment-law/SKILL.md).

## Ongoing calendar

| Obligation | Deadline |
|---|---|
| **Annual return (Form 15)** | First within 18 months of incorporation, then annually |
| Annual Company Registration Levy | Annually |
| Income tax return | Within 8 months of financial year end; **e-filing mandatory for companies** |
| VAT returns | Monthly or quarterly per registration category |
| EPF/ETF remittance | Monthly |
| APIT remittance | Monthly |
| Beneficial ownership updates | On any change |
| Director/secretary changes | Form 20 |
| Registered office change | Form 13 |

Late annual returns carry fines — reported at up to **LKR 100,000 for the company and
LKR 50,000 per director**, so directors are personally exposed. Verify current penalty levels
with the ROC.

## The first-year mistakes people make

1. Treating the annual return as optional until the company is "really trading"
2. Missing the beneficial ownership filing entirely because it's new
3. Bringing foreign equity in outside an IIA, then discovering repatriation problems
4. Not registering for EPF/ETF because staff are "on probation" or "consultants"
5. Registering for VAT voluntarily without understanding the monthly filing burden
6. Never holding a board meeting or keeping minutes, then needing them for a bank or investor
