# Incorporation checklist

## Step 1 — Name reservation

Search and reserve through **eROC**. An approved name is reserved for **three months**.

Names are refused if identical or too similar to an existing name, or if they imply state
backing or a regulated activity the company isn't licensed for. Expect the Registrar to ask
what an abbreviation stands for.

**Foreign-owned companies** need the name in **Sinhala, Tamil and English** in the Articles.
Sort the translations before you draft.

## Step 2 — Assemble the people

| Role | Minimum | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders | 1 | Max 50 for a private company. Individuals or corporates. |
| Directors | 1 | Must consent personally via Form 18. Natural persons. |
| Company Secretary | 1 | **Must hold a valid Certificate of Practice from the ROC.** A sole director cannot also be the secretary. |

Foreign directors and shareholders: certified passport copies.

## Step 3 — Prepare the documents

| Document | Who signs | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 1** — Application for registration | Per form | **eROC system-generated** |
| **Form 18** — Consent and certificate of director | **Each director, personally.** Two directors = two Form 18s | eROC-generated for first incorporation |
| **Form 19** — Consent and certificate of secretary | The Company Secretary | eROC-generated for first incorporation |
| **Articles of Association** | All shareholders (last page) | Model Articles (First Schedule) or bespoke |

### Form 1 captures

- Proposed company name and approval reference
- Registered office address in Sri Lanka
- Details of every initial shareholder and their shareholding
- Details of every initial director
- Company Secretary details

### Articles of Association

Either adopt the **model Articles in the First Schedule** to the Companies Act, or draft your
own. Sri Lanka has no memorandum — the Articles are the whole constitution.

Draft bespoke Articles if there is more than one shareholder. Things the model Articles will
not do for you: pre-emption on transfers, tag/drag rights, investor consent matters, board
appointment rights, deadlock resolution, anti-dilution.

If you draft your own, include a detailed objects/activity description — the Registrar
expects it, and the description tends to be reused by banks and licensing authorities.

## Step 4 — File

Submit through eROC with card payment.

**Rejection causes, in rough order of frequency:**
- Handwriting on a system-generated form
- Signature not visible after scanning
- Not A4, or not PDF
- A director signing another director's Form 18
- Name approval expired
- Secretary's Certificate of Practice lapsed

Typical turnaround is a few working days in normal conditions. Track status on the eROC
dashboard; directors and the secretary also receive confirmation emails.

## Step 5 — Certificate of incorporation

**Physical certificates are no longer issued.** Download the certificate from the eROC
dashboard. Directors and the secretary all have dashboard access.

## Step 6 — Public notice

A notice of incorporation must be published. The channel and timeframe have changed over
time — confirm the current requirement with the ROC or your Company Secretary rather than
relying on older guidance.

## Step 7 — Certified copies

Order certified copies of Form 1, Form 18, Form 19 and the Articles. Banks, the IRD, the BOI
and most licensing authorities ask for them, and getting them later is slower than getting
them now.

## Fees

Fees change. The ROC publishes the current table at https://drc.gov.lk/en/?page_id=11 —
check there rather than relying on figures quoted in blog posts, which are frequently stale
and often quote an outdated VAT rate on top.

Budget for: name reservation, Form 1, Form 18 (per director), Form 19, Articles, and certified
copies.
