# Breach and remedies

## Remedies available

Under Roman-Dutch law as applied in Sri Lanka:

| Remedy | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Specific performance** | Compelling performance. More readily available in Roman-Dutch law than under English law, where it is exceptional. |
| **Damages** | Compensation for loss caused by the breach |
| **Cancellation / rescission** | Terminating the contract, generally for material breach |
| **Interdict (injunction)** | Restraining conduct — interim and final |
| **Declaration** | Determining rights |

**Specific performance is a genuine difference from English law.** Do not assume a Sri Lankan
court will default to damages. Verify the current approach and its limits.

## Damages

Verify the Sri Lankan position on:

- **Causation and remoteness** — how the Sri Lankan courts frame recoverable loss
- **Mitigation** — the innocent party's duty to limit loss
- Recovery of **lost profits**
- Whether and when **non-pecuniary** loss is recoverable

## Liquidated damages and penalties

A clause fixing a sum payable on breach is enforceable if it is a genuine pre-estimate of
loss, and vulnerable if it is a penalty designed to compel performance.

Draft with the pre-estimate framing, and record the reasoning at the time of drafting — it is
much easier to defend a figure you can explain.

Verify the current Sri Lankan approach, which may not track English developments.

## Limitation / prescription

**Verify the applicable prescription period under the Prescription Ordinance before doing
anything else.** Different periods apply to different classes of claim, and the period may be
shorter than a party expects.

A time-barred claim is dead on the merits. This is the first thing to check when someone
brings you a dispute about an old invoice or an old agreement.

## Practical enforcement route

1. **Check prescription.** First, always.
2. **Review the contract** — is there a dispute resolution clause, a notice requirement, a cure
   period? Failing to follow a contractual escalation process can prejudice the claim.
3. **Letter of demand** from an Attorney-at-Law. Frequently resolves matters; also establishes
   a record.
4. **Negotiation or mediation.**
5. **Arbitration**, if the contract provides for it.
6. **Litigation** — Commercial High Court for significant commercial claims.

## Debt claims specifically

For straightforward debt recovery, and particularly for lenders, there is a summary route
under the **Debt Recovery (Special Provisions) Act No. 2 of 1990**. See
[`../../dispute-resolution/references/debt-recovery.md`](../../dispute-resolution/references/debt-recovery.md).

## Interim relief

Where assets may be dissipated or irreparable harm is threatened, interim relief may be
available. This is urgent, evidence-heavy work — get an Attorney-at-Law involved immediately
rather than gathering evidence first.

## Realism about timelines

Sri Lankan civil litigation is slow. A commercial dispute can take years to final judgment.
This should inform both the drafting decision (arbitration clause) and the dispute decision
(settle, or fight).

Factor it into commercial reasoning: a claim that is worth pursuing on paper may not be worth
pursuing once time, cost and management distraction are priced in.
