# Formation, execution and stamp duty

## Formation

Under Roman-Dutch law as applied in Sri Lanka, a contract generally requires:

- **Consensus** — agreement on the essential terms
- **Capacity** — the parties are legally able to contract
- **Lawful object** — not contrary to law or public policy
- **Possibility of performance**
- **Compliance with any required formalities**

Roman-Dutch law does not treat consideration the way English law does; the emphasis is on
serious intention to be bound. This matters for gratuitous promises and variations — do not
assume an English-law analysis transfers cleanly. Verify against current Sri Lankan authority.

## Oral and informal agreements

Most commercial contracts require **no particular form**. An oral agreement, an email exchange
or a WhatsApp thread can constitute a binding contract if the elements are present.

The practical problem is not validity but **proof**. Terms that were never written down become
whatever the witnesses can persuade a court they were. Reduce anything of value to writing.

## When writing or attestation is required

Certain transactions require writing, and some require **notarial attestation** before a Notary
Public under the **Prevention of Frauds Ordinance No. 7 of 1840** — most importantly
transactions concerning **land**.

A Notary Public in Sri Lanka holds a distinct statutory office with substantive functions, not
the light-touch notarisation familiar from some other jurisdictions. **Land transactions are
out of scope for this project** and require a Notary and an Attorney-at-Law.

Verify the full list of transactions requiring writing or attestation before assuming a
commercial contract is form-free.

## Electronic signatures

The **Electronic Transactions Act, No. 19 of 2006** gives legal recognition to electronic
records and signatures. Verify:

- the conditions for a valid electronic signature
- **which transactions are excluded** from the Act — exclusions typically cover land,
  wills, and other instruments requiring notarial execution
- evidentiary treatment

Practically: routine commercial contracts can be signed electronically. Anything notarial
cannot. Where the counterparty is a bank, a regulator or a government department, check what
they will actually accept, which is often narrower than the law permits.

## Corporate execution

Before signing, confirm:

- **Who is authorised** under the Articles to bind the company
- Whether a **board resolution** is needed, and obtain it
- Whether a **shareholders' agreement** makes this a reserved matter requiring consent
- Whether the **company seal** is required by the Articles
- That the signatory's name matches the ROC record (check the last Form 20)

A contract signed by someone without authority is a real risk in Sri Lankan practice,
particularly where a company has changed directors without filing Form 20.

## Stamp duty

Payable on specified **instruments** under the Stamp Duty (Special Provisions) Act No. 12 of
2006 and applicable regulations.

**Verify before execution:**
- [ ] Whether this instrument is chargeable
- [ ] The current rate and computation basis
- [ ] Who bears it (contractually and by default)
- [ ] When it must be paid
- [ ] The consequences of non-payment or late payment

Instruments commonly chargeable include **share transfers**, leases, and certain financial
instruments. Rates change; do not quote a figure from memory or from an undated source.

An unstamped or under-stamped instrument creates evidentiary problems and surfaces during
diligence, often years later at an inconvenient moment. Stamp at execution.
