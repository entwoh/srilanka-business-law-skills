# Directors' duties and liability

## The core duties

The Companies Act No. 7 of 2007 codifies directors' duties along lines familiar from
Commonwealth company law. Verify the precise statutory formulation and section numbers, but
the substance is:

- **Act in good faith and in the best interests of the company** — the company, not the
  shareholder who appointed you. This bites hardest on nominee directors appointed by an
  investor or a parent.
- **Exercise powers for a proper purpose** — issuing shares to dilute a rival shareholder is
  the classic improper purpose.
- **Comply with the Act and the Articles.**
- **Exercise the care, diligence and skill of a reasonable director** in the circumstances,
  taking account of the nature of the company and the director's position and responsibilities.
- **Not to agree to the company incurring obligations** it cannot reasonably be expected to
  perform.
- **Avoid reckless trading** — carrying on business in a manner likely to create substantial
  risk of serious loss to creditors.
- **Disclose interests** in transactions, and comply with the interested-director provisions.

## Personal liability

Directors of Sri Lankan companies face personal exposure in several distinct ways, which
people consistently underestimate:

| Source | Exposure |
|---|---|
| Late annual return | Reported at up to LKR 50,000 **per director**, separate from the company fine |
| Beneficial ownership non-compliance | **Criminal** exposure for officers personally |
| Reckless trading / improper obligations | Personal liability for company debts |
| Unpaid EPF/ETF | Officers can be pursued |
| Tax defaults | Officer liability in defined circumstances |
| Breach of duty | Action by the company, or derivative action |

The pattern is worth stating plainly to clients: **limited liability protects shareholders,
not directors who default on statutory obligations.**

## Interested transactions

Where a director has an interest in a transaction, disclosure is required and the interests
register must be updated. The Articles and the Act determine whether the interested director
may vote or count in the quorum.

Related-party transactions in a company with outside investors will typically also be caught
by consent provisions in a shareholders' agreement — check that as well as the Act.

## Nominee directors — the structural tension

A director appointed by an investor or a parent company owes duties **to the company**, not
to their appointer. When those interests diverge, the statutory duty governs. This is genuinely
difficult in practice and is a live issue in any company with investor board representation.

Practical mitigations: clear conflict protocols, abstention on conflicted matters, and
reserved-matter mechanics in the shareholders' agreement rather than reliance on the nominee's
vote.

## Appointment, resignation and removal

- Appointment: consent via **Form 18**; notify on **Form 20**
- Resignation: notify on **Form 20**. A resignation not filed leaves you on the public record
  as a director, and exposed accordingly
- Removal: a shareholder decision; check the Act and the Articles for the majority and
  procedure, including any right to be heard

**Resigning directors: confirm the Form 20 was actually filed.** People resign, assume the
company will file, and remain of record for years.

## Disqualification

Verify the statutory grounds. Broadly they cover undischarged bankrupts, persons convicted of
relevant offences, and persons disqualified by court order.
