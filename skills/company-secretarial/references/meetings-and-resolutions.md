# Meetings and resolutions

## The first question is always: what do the Articles say?

The Companies Act supplies defaults. The Articles override many of them — notice periods,
quorum, chairperson's casting vote, proxy rules, written resolution mechanics. Bespoke
Articles drafted for an investor round almost always add consent thresholds beyond the
statutory ones.

**Never answer a meetings question from the Act alone.** Read the Articles.

## Types of decision

| Decision by | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Board resolution** | Day-to-day management, operational matters, banking, appointing officers, calling shareholder meetings |
| **Ordinary resolution** (simple majority of votes cast) | Most shareholder decisions — appointing directors, approving accounts, appointing auditors |
| **Special resolution** (enhanced majority) | Fundamental changes — **amending the Articles**, changing the name, reduction of stated capital, major structural changes |

Confirm the statutory majority for a special resolution and any Articles-imposed higher
threshold before proceeding.

**A special resolution requires a Form 39 filing** (notice of a special resolution).

## Amending the Articles — the common error

Amending the Articles is a **shareholder** decision requiring a **special resolution**, and the
special resolution must be notified to the ROC on **Form 39**.

A board resolution purporting to amend the Articles is ineffective. This causes real problems
in investment transactions, where amended Articles are typically a condition precedent and the
investor's lawyers will check the resolution and the filing.

Where an amendment is contested between shareholders, the votes are what they are — the
threshold cannot be worked around by consent of the board or by the company secretary's
preference. Resolve it as a shareholder matter.

## Annual General Meeting

Check the current statutory position on AGM requirements for private companies, including
whether and when an AGM may be dispensed with, and what business must be transacted.

Typical AGM business: accounts, auditors, directors, dividends.

## Notice

Notice requirements to verify:
- Period of notice for ordinary and special business
- To whom notice must go (all shareholders, auditors, directors)
- Content requirements — a special resolution must be identified as such in the notice
- Whether short notice is permitted with consent, and at what threshold

Defective notice is a standard ground for challenging a resolution. Get it right.

## Quorum and proxies

Verify the statutory default quorum and whether the Articles alter it. Confirm proxy rights,
the form of proxy, and lodgement deadlines.

## Written resolutions

Sri Lankan practice makes heavy use of written resolutions signed by shareholders in lieu of
a meeting, which is a practical necessity for companies with a handful of shareholders.
Confirm the statutory basis, the required proportion of signatures, and any excluded matters.

## Minutes

Keep minutes of every board and shareholder meeting in the minute book. Minutes should record:

- date, time, place (or means, if held remotely)
- attendees and quorum
- declarations of interest
- the resolution as passed, in full
- the result of the vote

An undocumented decision is very hard to prove later — to a bank, to an investor's lawyers,
to the Registrar, or to a court. In practice, missing minutes are one of the most common
diligence findings in Sri Lankan transactions.
