# Statutory registers and records

## What must be kept

| Record | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Register of shareholders** | Every shareholder, holdings, dates of entry and cessation |
| **Register of directors** | Every director, particulars, appointment and cessation dates |
| **Register of secretaries** | Company Secretary particulars |
| **Register of charges** | Charges created over company assets (Form 11 is the prescribed form) |
| **Beneficial ownership register** | Beneficial owners and authorised person — since 30 March 2026 |
| **Minute book** | Minutes of all board and shareholder meetings, and written resolutions |
| **Accounting records** | Sufficient to explain transactions and show the company's position |
| **Interests register** | Directors' declared interests, where required |

## Where they're kept

Records are normally kept at the registered office. If kept elsewhere, notify the ROC:

- **Form 14** — notice of change of location of the records and registers
- **Form 16** — notice of location of accounting records

These are routinely forgotten when a company moves office or changes accountants, and the
mismatch then surfaces in the annual return.

## The register of shareholders

This is the one that matters most in practice. It is the primary evidence of who owns the
company. Share certificates are secondary; the register governs.

Update it on:

- every allotment of new shares (and file **Form 6**)
- every transfer of existing shares
- every transmission on death or insolvency
- every buy-back or redemption (and file **Form 9**)

An out-of-date shareholder register is the most common finding in Sri Lankan transaction
diligence, and the most disruptive, because it calls into question who actually has the votes.

## Keeping the chain intact

For any share transaction, the paper chain should be complete and consistent:

1. Board or shareholder resolution authorising it
2. Minute recording the decision
3. Share transfer form or allotment documentation, properly stamped where applicable
4. Register of shareholders updated
5. Share certificate issued or cancelled
6. ROC form filed (Form 6 for issues)
7. Beneficial ownership register reviewed and updated
8. Reflected in the next annual return

A gap at any step is a gap someone will find later.

## Inspection rights

Shareholders have statutory rights to inspect certain registers. Refusing a proper inspection
request is itself a breach and tends to escalate an ordinary shareholder disagreement into a
formal dispute. Verify the specific inspection provisions and notice requirements.

## Retention

Keep records for the statutory period; in practice keep them indefinitely. Accounting records
carry their own retention requirements under tax law, which are longer than most people assume.

## Practical advice

- Keep registers in a form that can be printed and signed, not only in an accounting package
- Scan the minute book — original minute books are lost surprisingly often
- Review registers annually as part of the Form 15 preparation, not as a separate exercise
- After any transaction, reconcile the register against the ROC record before closing
