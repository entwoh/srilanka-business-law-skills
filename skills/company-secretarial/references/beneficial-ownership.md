# Beneficial ownership (UBO) register

> **This is the most commonly missed obligation in Sri Lankan company compliance right now.**
> It is new, it applies to every company, it carries criminal exposure for officers
> personally, and many companies incorporated before 2026 have not acted on it.
> Raise it proactively.

## The legal basis

- **Companies (Amendment) Act, No. 12 of 2025** — inserted a new division, **ss 130A–130J**,
  into the Companies Act No. 7 of 2007
- **Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulation No. 1 of 2026** — Extraordinary Gazette
  **No. 2480/48 of 21 March 2026**
- **In force: 30 March 2026**

The reform aligns Sri Lanka with FATF standards on anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist
financing, and met an IMF structural benchmark. That context matters: enforcement pressure is
external and sustained, not discretionary.

## Who is a beneficial owner

A **natural person** who ultimately owns or controls **10% or more** of a company, whether
through:

- direct ownership,
- indirect ownership,
- control of shares,
- voting rights,
- other ownership interests, or
- exercising **effective control**.

**"Effective control"** expressly includes:

- control exercised indirectly through a chain of ownership
- the ability to appoint or remove a director
- the capacity to make strategic decisions affecting the company's operations or general
  direction

The point is to trace through holding companies, trusts and nominee arrangements to the real
individuals. A corporate shareholder is never the answer — you must go up the chain until you
reach natural persons.

## What companies must do

1. **Identify** the beneficial owners, tracing through any corporate or nominee layers.
2. **Maintain a beneficial ownership register** with the prescribed particulars.
3. **Appoint an authorised person** — a natural person **resident in Sri Lanka** responsible
   for safekeeping the register and making details available to authorities. Notified using
   **Form BO 5**.
4. **File with the ROC** and keep the filing current as ownership changes.

## Deadlines

| Company | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **New incorporations** | Applies immediately from incorporation — no grace period |
| **Companies existing at 30 March 2026** | Transitional window to file. Reported as **six months from the operative date** (i.e. by around 30 September 2026). One source reports 30 days for the authorised person appointment. |
| **Depositaries of a licensed stock exchange** | Notify the Registrar of all shareholders holding 10%+ within 30 days of the operative date |

> ⚠️ **Conflicting reporting on the transitional deadline.** Credible sources variously
> describe a six-month window for existing companies and a 30-day window for certain
> notifications. The Regulation text and the ROC's own notices are authoritative — check
> **Gazette No. 2480/48** and https://drc.gov.lk/en/?page_id=4607 directly before advising
> anyone on timing. Given the date of the operative period, treat this as **urgent**.

## Who can see it

**On request to the company or the ROC**, full details are available to:

- the Attorney-General
- the Financial Intelligence Unit
- the Director-General of Customs
- the Commissioner-General of Inland Revenue
- public authorities investigating criminal offences
- public procurement authorities
- regulatory authorities

**Publicly**, the ROC makes available a limited set: full names, nationalities, countries of
residence, business addresses, and the nature and extent of the beneficial ownership.

So: the identity of your 10%+ owners is now a matter of public record, in outline. This is a
real change for structures that relied on nominee arrangements for privacy.

## Consequences of non-compliance

Officers face **personal criminal exposure**, not merely company-level fines. Late compliance
is treated on the same footing as non-compliance. Confirm the precise penalty provisions in
ss 130A–130J before advising.

## Practical effect on transactions

Beneficial ownership is now a standard diligence item in Sri Lankan M&A and investment:

- Buyers ask for the BO register and the ROC filing as a condition precedent
- Banks request it for KYC and account opening
- A gap in the BO chain will stall a transaction
- Nominee and trust structures need to be explained, not hidden

If you are working on a share issue or transfer, the BO position changes with it — update the
register as part of closing, not afterwards. See
[`../../shares-and-capital/SKILL.md`](../../shares-and-capital/SKILL.md).

## Checklist

- [ ] Every beneficial owner identified, tracing through all corporate and nominee layers
- [ ] Anyone with 10%+ of shares *or* votes captured
- [ ] Anyone with effective control captured, even below 10% shareholding
- [ ] Register opened with prescribed particulars
- [ ] Authorised person appointed — natural person, resident in Sri Lanka
- [ ] Form BO 5 filed
- [ ] ROC filing made within the applicable window
- [ ] Process in place to update on any ownership or control change
- [ ] BO position reviewed as part of any share transaction
