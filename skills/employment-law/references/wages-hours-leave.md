# Wages, hours, leave and holidays

## Which statute applies

Sri Lanka has no single employment code. The applicable rules depend on the workplace:

| Workplace | Principal statute |
|---|---|
| Shops and offices | **Shop and Office Employees Act No. 19 of 1954** |
| Factories | Factories Ordinance |
| Trades covered by a Wages Board | **Wages Boards Ordinance** decisions for that trade |
| All workers | National Minimum Wage Act No. 3 of 2016 |

Wages Board decisions set trade-specific minimum wages and conditions that can exceed the
general position. Check whether the employer's trade has one.

## Minimum wage

| | Amount | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| National minimum wage | **LKR 30,000 per month** | 1 January 2026 |
| Daily rate | **LKR 1,200 per day** | 1 January 2026 |

The 2026 revision folded previous budgetary relief allowances into the new base wage, and
applies to employees hired **directly or through contractors** — so using a labour contractor
does not avoid it. The Department of Labour oversees compliance.

A Wages Board decision for a particular trade may set a higher minimum. The national figure
is a floor, not a target.

## Working hours (shop and office employees)

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Daily | **9 hours**, including meal and rest intervals |
| Weekly | **45 hours** |
| Typical pattern | Five and a half days |
| Weekly rest | One whole day plus one half-day |

## Overtime

Overtime must be paid at **not less than 1.5 times** the normal hourly rate.

Verify current statutory caps on overtime hours (daily, weekly and annual limits apply) and
any sector variations.

## Leave

Entitlements to verify against the Shop and Office Employees Act and any applicable Wages
Board decision:

- **Annual leave** — accrual basis and entitlement, which depends on service in the preceding year
- **Casual leave**
- **Sick leave** — note there is **no separate statutory sick-pay scheme** for shop and office
  staff; sick leave sits within the statutory leave framework
- **Maternity leave** — under the Maternity Benefits Ordinance, **employer-funded**. The day
  count is set by statute and **varies with the number of prior live births**, so confirm the
  entitlement case by case rather than applying one number
- **Paternity leave** — there is **no separate statutory paternity leave** for private-sector
  employees; any entitlement is contractual

**Accrued but untaken annual leave must be paid out on termination.**

## Public holidays

Sri Lanka has an unusually high number of public holidays — around **26 gazetted days**,
including roughly **12–13 Poya (full moon) days** annually, alongside mercantile holidays such
as the Sinhala and Tamil New Year, May Day and Christmas.

This materially affects productive days per year and should be factored into project
planning and cost models for anyone comparing Sri Lanka with other markets.

## Wage payment

- Wages must be paid at least **monthly**
- Detailed **wage records** must be maintained
- Lawful deductions only. **ETF can never be deducted from the employee.**

## Bonuses

There is **no mandatory 13th-month salary** in Sri Lanka. Bonuses are contractual. Many
employers pay a customary annual bonus, often around the April new year — if it has been paid
consistently, consider whether it has become an implied contractual term before withdrawing it.
