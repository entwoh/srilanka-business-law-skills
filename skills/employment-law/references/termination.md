# Termination, TEWA and Labour Tribunals

> Treat every live termination as significant. Sri Lanka is **not** an at-will jurisdiction,
> the employee-protective bias of the system is real, and a Labour Tribunal has very broad
> discretion. Get an Attorney-at-Law involved before acting, not after.

## The central rule — TEWA

Under the **Termination of Employment of Workmen (Special Provisions) Act, No. 45 of 1971**,
an employer with **15 or more employees** may not terminate a workman's employment without:

- the **employee's written consent**, or
- the **prior written approval of the Commissioner of Labour**,

**unless** the termination is for **proven misconduct** following a proper disciplinary inquiry.

That exception is narrower than employers assume. "Poor performance", "restructuring", "the
role is no longer needed" and "it isn't working out" are **not** misconduct.

## Which route applies

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| Proven misconduct after proper inquiry | Disciplinary dismissal — no Commissioner approval needed, but the inquiry must be genuine |
| Redundancy / restructuring | **Commissioner approval** or employee consent |
| Poor performance | **Commissioner approval** or employee consent |
| Employee resigns genuinely | Resignation — but watch for constructive dismissal |
| Mutual separation | Employee's **written consent**, properly documented and paid |
| Fixed term genuinely expiring | Verify — repeated renewals can create an expectation of continuity |
| Employer has fewer than 15 employees | TEWA does not apply, but Labour Tribunal jurisdiction may still |

## The disciplinary inquiry

Where dismissal is for misconduct, the inquiry is what makes it defensible. At minimum:

1. **Written charges**, specific enough for the employee to answer
2. Adequate **time to prepare**
3. A **hearing** before an impartial inquiring officer
4. The employee's right to **be heard**, to present evidence, and to be represented as
   permitted
5. Evidence actually **tested**, not merely asserted
6. A **reasoned written finding**
7. A penalty **proportionate** to the misconduct

A tribunal will look at both the substance (did it happen?) and the process (was it fair?).
A genuine dismissal for real misconduct routinely fails on process.

## Commissioner approval

Where TEWA applies and there is no consent, apply to the Commissioner of Labour for approval
**before** terminating. The Commissioner may approve, refuse, or approve subject to payment of
compensation.

**Compensation ordered by the Commissioner is capped at LKR 2,500,000 per workman.** Verify
the current cap and the formula, which is typically based on years of service.

The process takes time. Build that into any restructuring plan — a redundancy programme
cannot be executed on a Western timetable.

## Mutual separation

Often the most practical route. The employee consents in writing, in exchange for an agreed
package. To hold up, it should be:

- genuinely voluntary, without pressure that could be characterised as coercion
- documented in a written agreement
- supported by consideration beyond bare statutory entitlements
- inclusive of all statutory dues actually paid — EPF, ETF, gratuity, accrued leave, notice

A "resignation" extracted under pressure can be recharacterised as termination. If the
employee later says they were forced out, the surrounding facts will decide it.

## Labour Tribunals

An individual employee claiming unjust termination applies to a **Labour Tribunal** under the
Industrial Disputes Act No. 43 of 1950.

What makes tribunals distinctive:

- Jurisdiction to make an order that is **"just and equitable"** — broader than strict legal
  right
- Remedies include **reinstatement with back wages**, or compensation
- Procedure is less formal than a court
- The tribunal can look behind the form of what happened to its substance

The breadth of "just and equitable" is the key point. An employer may be technically correct
and still lose, if the tribunal considers the outcome unfair.

## What must be paid on any termination

- Salary to the last day worked
- **Accrued but untaken annual leave**
- **EPF and ETF** up to date, including the final month
- **Gratuity**, if the employer has 15+ and the employee has 5+ completed years
- Notice or payment in lieu, per contract and statute
- Any contractual entitlements — bonus, commission, notice enhancements

Pay these promptly. Late gratuity attracts surcharges, and unpaid dues make a tribunal
significantly less sympathetic to the employer on everything else.

## Resignation

An employee resigning must give contractual notice. An employer cannot generally refuse to
accept a resignation, and should not withhold statutory dues to pressure an employee into
staying — doing so converts an ordinary departure into a claim.

## Practical sequence for a contemplated termination

1. Count employees over the preceding twelve months — is TEWA engaged?
2. Characterise it honestly: misconduct, or not?
3. If misconduct → run a proper inquiry. Do not shortcut it.
4. If not misconduct → seek written consent (mutual separation) **or** apply to the
   Commissioner. Do not terminate first.
5. Compute all statutory dues, including gratuity
6. Take advice before communicating anything to the employee
7. Document everything contemporaneously
